# Chapter One

The light came on at dusk, as it had every evening for a hundred and twelve years, and for a moment the whole cape seemed to hold its breath.

Marta climbed the last of the hundred and four steps with the logbook pressed against her chest. The wind had turned during the afternoon; she could feel it in the glass, a faint shiver that ran through the lantern room like a thought nobody wanted to finish.

Below her, the sea was the colour of slate. Somewhere beyond the reef a trawler was making for the harbour, its deck lights swinging, and she wrote the time in the margin out of habit — *18:42, northbound, in a hurry* — before she let herself look at the horizon properly.

There was a storm in it. Not tonight, perhaps not tomorrow, but it was there, folded into the edge of the world like a letter that had not yet been opened.

She had learned, in thirty-one winters, that the sea always tells you first. The trick is to be listening.
